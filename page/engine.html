Turn a folder of documents into a question-answering cell you can check.

Genome Fold reads your documents (plain text or markdown) and builds a small, self-contained answerer from them. It uses no language model. Every answer is a sentence of your documents, copied word for word, with the passage it came from and its exact character positions, so anyone can check it. Questions outside the documents' subject are refused. The result ships as its own repository only when the estate genome's birth check admits it.

How it works

Seven steps, all of them small enough to check by hand. The same code runs in this page and on the command line: the page carries the kernel's files byte for byte, and its test fails if they ever differ.

  1. Passages. Each document is cut at blank lines and headings. A heading becomes the title of the passages under it.
  2. Index. Every word of every passage is counted, so the cell knows which words are rare (they matter more) and which are common.
  3. Answerer. For a question, the passage that best carries the question's words wins (a standard word-overlap score, BM25), and in it the sentence that carries the most of them. Its exact character positions are found with DocDec's own span finder.
  4. Judge. An answer record is verified only when its text is exactly the passage's characters at those positions and that passage uses enough of the question's words. Change one word and it is not verified. A record that is not an answer record is refused.
  5. Lane. A question is in the cell's lane when enough of its words are words the documents use. "Enough" is chosen, not guessed: the engine writes questions from the documents' own rarest words, takes a fixed set of everyday off-topic questions, picks the share that best separates the two on half of them, and measures it on the other half.
  6. Cases and seed. The judge's good case is a real sentence record the cell verifies; its bad case is the same record with one word altered. The engine drafts the cell's seed.json: what it is, its lane, its judge, its hooks and where it came from.
  7. Birth check. The estate genome's check runs the cell's own hooks: it must describe itself, its judge must tell the good case from the bad, its health and reversible tombstone must work, and its lane must take the in-lane sample and refuse the off-topic one. Only then is the folder written.

A real run: the Consumer Rights Act 2015, Part 1

Measured, and pre-registered

Before any number was computed, the rules were written down and committed (PREREG.md). The test set is DocDec's S1 set: 130 Wikipedia paragraphs from SQuAD 2.0 (Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, 2018; CC BY-SA 4.0), with 420 held-out questions about them and 420 off-topic ones. The engine never saw any of these questions: its only tuned number, the lane share, came from its own generated questions. DocDec v2's published numbers on the same questions sit beside each one.

#WhatThis cellDocDec v2 (published)RuleResult

The no-model cell finds the right passage more often than DocDec's cosine baseline and refuses nearly all off-topic questions, in well under a millisecond. It answers fewer questions than DocDec v2 does, and it does not refuse SQuAD's unanswerable questions: they are written to look answerable from a real paragraph, and a word-overlap lane cannot tell. This cell has no reader and no 34 MB model; DocDec v2 has both. The comparison shows what checking without a model buys, not that it does DocDec's job. CI re-derives every number here except the timing, from the shipped data and the upstream file.

Blueprint

Five organs, built and gated one at a time, plus the demo corpus's converter. Every file of logic is mutation-tested by witness v0.6: each decision point is flipped in turn and the tests must notice. CLEAN means every flip was caught, with no exemptions.

A folded cell holds: seed.json; data/ (the passages, the lane measurement, the generated questions); corpus/ (the source files, byte for byte); kernel/ (this kernel and the pieces it composes, byte for byte); genome/ (the hooks, including act.mjs, which answers a question); the birth-check workflow; FOLD.json (the engine commit and every hash); its own page; and its licences. The engine itself is a cell too: its seed.json and genome/ pass the same birth check in its own CI.

What it does for your business

Most organisations have documents people keep asking about: policies, contracts, product manuals, regulations, onboarding guides. Genome Fold turns each set into an answerer whose every answer can be traced to a sentence and checked, and which says no rather than guessing. It is built for places where a wrong-but-confident answer costs more than no answer.

Chief executive

Answers from your own documents that can be shown to a regulator, a customer or a court: the sentence, the document, the position. No model to trust, no outside service to depend on.

Finance

No per-seat licence and no model hosting bill to run it: it answers in well under a millisecond on an ordinary laptop (measured: ms a question, against s for a local language-model reader). We quote no money saving here, because it depends on your volumes.

Operations

One cell per document set, each with its own lane: the HR handbook cell refuses supplier questions and says who to ask instead. Re-folding after an update is one command, and the birth check runs again.

Technology and security

Runs entirely where you put it: a browser tab or a Node process. No network calls, no data leaves the machine, no model weights. The code is small enough to read, mutation-tested, and every cell's CI re-runs its birth check.

Compliance and audit

Every answer is a record of source, start and end. Anyone can check it against the file. The judge that verifies it is the same one the birth check tests on a known-bad record.

The teams who answer questions

A first pass that points to the exact sentence, so people read one paragraph instead of a whole manual, and a refusal when the answer is not in the documents at all.

Rolling it out

  1. Pick one document set that people ask about every week. Export it as markdown or text.
  2. Fold it. Read the lane numbers and the judge's cases the engine prints. If the birth check refuses the cell, the list says why.
  3. Ask it the twenty questions your team gets most. Note where the verified sentence is not the best one (see the limits below).
  4. Push the cell as a repository. Its CI runs the birth check on every change; its page lets anyone ask it.
  5. Wire its act hook into your tools or agents: echo '{"question":"..."}' | node genome/act.mjs answers with a verified record or a refusal.

Straight answers to the hard questions

  • Is a verified answer a correct answer? No. Verified means the words are really in your documents at the stated position, and the passage uses enough of the question's words. On the public test set, of answerable questions got a verified sentence from the right passage. Treat it as a pointer to read, not a final word.
  • Will it refuse questions it cannot answer? Questions outside the subject, nearly always ( on the test set). Questions inside the subject that the documents do not answer, mostly not ( refused). That is the biggest limit.
  • Where does our data go? Nowhere. The fold and the answers run on your machine or in your browser.
  • Who supports it? Supplied by AI Native Solutions. Support is agreed per deployment, in each deployment's agreement.
  • Who owns it? The code is MIT licensed. Your documents keep their own licence; a folded cell records it.

The case for the board

An answer you can check beats an answer you have to trust. Genome Fold gives each document set a small, inspectable answerer that cites itself and refuses outside its lane, with its weaknesses measured and published before anyone relies on it.

Limits

  • No reader and no language model: the answer is a whole sentence, not a short phrase, and finding it is word overlap.
  • No stemming: "boil" does not match "boils". Questions phrased in other words than the documents' are harder for it.
  • Verified is not the same as right: the best-supported sentence can be the wrong one (the real run above shows one).
  • Unanswerable questions inside the subject are mostly answered anyway (M4 failed: refused).
  • The off-topic set is fixed and in English. A corpus that is itself about everyday topics will find some of them in its lane; the engine then reports that, and the lane's measured numbers show it.
  • Plain text and markdown only. Convert other formats first (the Consumer Rights Act example converts legislation.gov.uk's XML).
  • In the browser, the birth check runs three of its four traits; the fourth (the reversible tombstone) needs files and runs when you fold on the command line and in the cell's CI.
  • Measured on one public benchmark of three Wikipedia articles, not on your documents.

The explainer: why fold, and what "verified" means

The problem. An estate of software parts needs each part to say what it is, to check its own work, to know its own lane and to be removable without harm. The estate genome's birth check tests exactly that. Making each answering part by hand, with its own judge and its own lane, is slow, and the hand-made judges vary in how strict they are.

The fold. Genome Fold makes the part from the documents. The judge is always the same strict one: exact characters at exact positions, plus enough of the question in that passage. The lane is always chosen and measured the same way, on questions written from the documents and a fixed off-topic set, and its numbers ship with the cell. The test cases the birth check needs are written by the engine from a real answer and a one-word alteration of it, so the judge is shown to catch the smallest possible change.

What the guarantee is. If a cell says verified, the sentence is in the cited document at the cited position, character for character, and that passage uses at least the lane's share of the question's words. That is checkable by anyone, with nothing but the file. What the guarantee is not: that the sentence answers the question well. The measurement says how often it does, and the page says it plainly.

Where it came from. The span finder, the sentence finder and the lane measurement are DocDec's, unchanged and pinned. The birth check and its hooks are the estate genome's, unchanged and pinned. The fold engine composes them; it adds the answerer, the judge's support rule, the question generator and the writer.
